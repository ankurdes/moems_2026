🧩 Brain teaser (5-10 min)

Don't just guess. Try a small case first, look for a pattern, then say why it works. Work in pairs if you like.

1The 100 lockers★★★★☆
A hallway has 100 lockers, numbered 1 to 100, all closed. Student 1 walks by and opens every locker. Student 2 walks by and flips every 2nd locker (opens it if closed, closes it if open). Student 3 flips every 3rd locker, and so on, until Student 100 flips only locker 100. After all 100 students have gone by, which lockers are open?
Hint: try it with just 10 lockers first. Then ask yourself: how many times does locker 12 get flipped, and by which students?

The perfect squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100 (10 lockers)
Locker n is flipped once by each student whose number divides n. For locker 12 that is students 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12, which is 6 flips, so it ends closed. A locker ends open only if it is flipped an odd number of times, meaning n has an odd number of divisors. Divisors come in pairs (like 3 and 4 for 12), except when a divisor pairs with itself, which happens only when n is a perfect square (10 × 10 = 100). Lesson: divisors come in pairs, and this is a preview of next week.
2Burning ropes (backup)★★★★☆
You have two ropes and a lighter. Each rope takes exactly 60 minutes to burn from one end to the other, but they burn unevenly (half the rope's length might take 10 minutes or 50 minutes). You have no clock. How can you measure exactly 45 minutes?
Light rope A at both ends and rope B at one end at the same time.
Rope A burning from both ends takes exactly 30 minutes, no matter how unevenly it burns. At that moment, light the other end of rope B. B has 30 minutes of burn left, and with two flames it finishes in 15 minutes. Total: 30 + 15 = 45 minutes. Lesson: lighting both ends halves the time, even if the rope is uneven.

📝 Diagnostic (25 min, solo)

5 problems, easy to hard. Don't worry about finishing everything. Do the ones you can, show your work, and be honest about what felt hard.

25:00
1Arithmetic / sequences★★☆☆☆
The sum of 7 consecutive odd integers is 217. What is the largest of the 7 integers?
37
The middle number is the average: 217 ÷ 7 = 31. The odd numbers are 25, 27, 29, 31, 33, 35, 37, so the largest is 37.
2Mixtures / percent★★★☆☆
A 40-liter mixture is 25% acid. How many liters of pure water must be added so that the mixture is 20% acid?
10 liters
The acid stays the same: 25% of 40 = 10 liters. For this to be 20% of the new total, the total must be 10 ÷ 0.20 = 50 liters. So add 50 − 40 = 10 liters of water.
3Counting★★★★☆
How many 4-digit numbers (1000 to 9999) have digits that add up to 5?
35
Do casework on the first digit. First digit 1: the other three digits add to 4, which can happen in 15 ways. First digit 2: other digits add to 3, 10 ways. First digit 3: 6 ways. First digit 4: 3 ways. First digit 5: 1 way. Total 15 + 10 + 6 + 3 + 1 = 35. (Shortcut: stars and bars, C(7,3) = 35.)
4Geometry★★★★☆
A right triangle has legs of length 9 and 12. What is the length of the altitude drawn from the right angle to the hypotenuse?
7.2 (or 36/5)
The hypotenuse is 15 (a 9-12-15 triangle, which is 3 times the 3-4-5). The area is ½ × 9 × 12 = 54. Also, area = ½ × 15 × h, so 15h = 108 and h = 7.2. Trick: the area can be found two ways.
5Number theory★★★★★
What is the smallest positive integer that has exactly 12 positive divisors?
60
If n = pa qb rc..., the number of divisors is (a+1)(b+1)(c+1)... To get 12 the exponents can be 11, or 5 and 1, or 3 and 2, or 2, 1 and 1. The smallest number in each case: 211 = 2048, 25·3 = 96, 23·32 = 72, 22·3·5 = 60. The smallest is 60.

🏠 Homework (optional)

Do any you like. We'll go over them at the start of next week.

1Patterns★★★☆☆
What is the units (ones) digit of 32027?
7
The units digits of powers of 3 repeat every 4: 3, 9, 7, 1, 3, 9, 7, 1, ... Since 2027 = 4 × 506 + 3, the digit is the 3rd in the cycle, which is 7.
2Counting / sets★★★☆☆
How many positive integers less than 100 are divisible by 3 or by 5, but not by both?
40
Multiples of 3 under 100: 33. Multiples of 5: 19. Multiples of 15 (both): 6. We want to exclude the "both" numbers completely, so take (33 − 6) + (19 − 6) = 27 + 13 = 40.
3Averages★★☆☆☆
The average of five numbers is 18. When one of the numbers is removed, the average of the remaining four numbers is 16. What number was removed?
26
The five numbers add up to 5 × 18 = 90. The remaining four add up to 4 × 16 = 64. The removed number is 90 − 64 = 26.
